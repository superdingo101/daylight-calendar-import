Daylight Calendar Import runs inside Home Assistant. The self-hosted integration needs **no Daylight Cloud account**, but it is not an entirely offline AI workflow: **your configured AI Task provider may receive the content you ask Daylight to process**.

## Where does information go?

<Steps>
  <Step title="Source enters Home Assistant">
    You paste an announcement, upload a file, or optionally let Direct IMAP read a message from your configured mailbox/folder. Private source context can be retained with pending drafts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your chosen AI Task provider interprets it">
    Source text, usable extracted PDF text, or supported media attachments may leave Home Assistant for that provider. Consult the provider's own billing, privacy, model-training, and retention policies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You review the proposed events">
    Titles, dates, destinations, and descriptions remain in local pending-review storage until the work is resolved or otherwise handled under the documented lifecycle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approval writes to your calendar">
    The destination calendar integration receives the event details. If that integration syncs with Google Calendar, CalDAV, or another external service, its separate privacy terms also apply.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Special care with email

Direct IMAP can access **all unread, undeleted messages** in the selected folder. Use a dedicated address or folder. An exact-sender filter restricts parsing of nonmatching messages but does **not** authenticate the sender and does not prevent Daylight from fetching headers. See [Optional email intake](/configuration/email).

## What is retained locally?

| Data | Local handling |
| --- | --- |
| Unresolved imports and uncertain calendar writes | Preserved until resolved; protected from normal completed-history trimming |
| Completed activity summaries | Pruned after **30 days** from last transition or beyond the **newest 500** completed records |
| Completed notification requests | Normally trimmed after **30 days** or beyond the **newest 1,000**; unresolved delivery is protected |
| Source/event deduplication fingerprints | Up to the **newest 10,000 in each history**, without a new age expiry |
| Temporary uploads | Removed after processing; pending context and summaries have separate lifecycles |

Some private source details may remain in pending records. A manually entered recurring source that produces no drafts can retain up to **4,096 characters** in authorized private activity detail so the user can handle the schedule manually; that detail is not exposed in notifications or diagnostic downloads. Completion dates and protected in-flight operations can affect pruning.

<Warning>
  **Do not delete Daylight's private storage to clear errors or duplicates.** That can discard uncertainty checkpoints or deduplication history and lead to duplicate calendar entries. Use [storage recovery](/storage-recovery) only when needed, with a private backup.
</Warning>

## Diagnostics and notifications

Daylight's native diagnostics export uses a redacted projection of operational counts and declared capabilities. It excludes original source text, event titles, mailbox details, credentials, and event identifiers. Home Assistant may include its own standard download metadata.

Optional push notifications are **off by default** and omit private event details. Notifications may be repeated after interrupted delivery; they do not guarantee a calendar write or display on the receiving device.

For complete exceptions to local retention, see the [local retention reference](/retention). Daylight does not control how long the external AI or calendar provider retains data.
